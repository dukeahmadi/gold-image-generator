# Image pipelines

Rule for every pipeline here: **the product's pixels are never redrawn.** A segmentation model finds the
product, its original pixels are copied onto a new background, and they are only ever scaled *down*
(never up), and only when several pieces or a small plate slot require it. Image models are used for one
thing only: painting *empty backgrounds* (plates) once, for a human to review. Text (brand name, price) is
rendered by code with real fonts, because image models misspell Persian.

| Type | Command | AI model involved | Product pixels |
|---|---|---|---|
| `white` | `render --types white` | no | unchanged |
| `solid` | `render --types solid --colors navy "#0B1F3A"` | no | unchanged |
| `set` | `render --types set` (all photos together) | no | unchanged, shrunk to a common size |
| `scene` | `render --types scene --plates plates` | background plates, generated once | unchanged |
| `accessories` | `render --types accessories --plates plates` | background plates with props | unchanged |
| `display` | `render --types display --plates plates` | background plates of compatible displays | unchanged |

Run everything with `python -m gold_imagegen.render PHOTOS... --types ... --out out/render`. Every photo is cut
out once (see README for the cutout model, memory and speed) and reused for all types.

**Not supported on purpose:** a necklace on a bust or mannequin. A flat-lay chain would have to change shape to
drape, which only a generative model can do, and that can change stones, engraving or metal color. It breaks the
rule above. See "Compatible displays" for what is possible instead.

## 1. White catalog (`white`)

Cutout -> square canvas, product centered with `margin=0.08`, soft shadow offset down-right. Layout is
`side = round(max(w, h) / (1 - 2 * margin))`. The product is never scaled.

## 2. Solid / brand color (`solid`)

Same layout on any color: a preset (beige, black, blush, burgundy, charcoal, emerald, navy, white) or a hex code. On dark colors
(luminance < 0.35) a soft vignette is added and the shadow is stronger; `--reflection` adds a glossy-floor
mirror image under the product instead of the shadow.

Observed on real photos: yellow gold pops on navy and black; it has little contrast on beige. Prefer dark colors
for gold, and check a light brand color before committing to it.

## 3. Multi-piece set (`set`)

All photos in the run go on one square canvas in a centered grid (`cols = ceil(sqrt(n))`, last row centered).
Pieces are shrunk to the longest side of the **smallest** piece, then multiplied by `--weights` (each in
(0, 1], one per photo, in order; e.g. `0.5` for earrings).

- The set's resolution is limited by the lowest-resolution photo, because nothing is enlarged.
- Relative sizes are **visual weight, not real proportions**: a photo cannot tell us how big a ring is next to a
  necklace. Pass `--weights` if the shop knows.

## 4-6. Plates: studio scene, accessories, compatible displays

A **plate** is a background image generated by a model *without any jewelry*, plus a small JSON file that says
where the product goes (`slot`, with `anchor` center/top/bottom), where the light comes from (`light`) and, for
cushions, where to hide the product's lower part (`occlude_below`). See `gold_imagegen/plates.py`.

Workflow:

1. `python -m gold_imagegen.make_plates --kind scene --style white_marble --count 4 --out plates`
   (needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`; `--max-cost` guards the budget; default model `black-forest-labs/flux-3-image`,
   square by default; each plate's prompt, model, cost and time are stored in its JSON).
2. **Look at every plate.** Delete any with objects in the middle, jewelry, text or odd perspective.
3. Adjust the JSON when needed (move `slot`, set `occlude_below` on a ring cushion).
4. `python -m gold_imagegen.render photo.jpg --types scene --plates plates`.

Plates are paid for once and reused for every product. The product is placed in the slot, centered, shrunk only if
it is bigger than the slot, with a soft shadow falling away from `light`.

### Studio scene (`scene`) and accessories (`accessories`)

Styles: grey_marble, linen, silk, stone, velvet, white_marble, wood. Props: baby_breath, gift, pearls, roses.
Plates are photographed **straight from above**, so the product photo should be a flat lay too. Props stay near
the edges and never touch the product (nothing is drawn over it).

### Compatible displays (`display`)

Kinds: `display_ring` (velvet cushion with a slit), `display_earrings` (display card), `display_box` (open box,
top-down). They only work when the **product photo already has the matching angle**: ring upright from the
front, earrings hanging from the front, items lying flat for the box. Set `occlude_below` on the ring cushion
so the ring's base disappears into the slit.

## Known risks (not yet measured with real AI plates)

- **Lighting and color mismatch.** The product keeps the light of its own photo; a cool marble plate under a
  warm-lit product can look pasted on. Plates ask for soft window light from the upper left; shoot product
  photos in similar soft light.
- **Shadow realism.** The shadow is a blurred, offset silhouette. It is fine for flat items; a pendant that
  stands up needs a tighter contact shadow.
- **Plate resolution** (about 1-2 MP from current models) caps the output size. Larger product photos are
  shrunk to the slot.
- **Plate quality is a model lottery**, hence the human review step.

## What has been checked

- Unit tests (68), including exact preservation of opaque product pixels for white, solid, reflection, vignette,
  sets and plates, and the slot anchors.
- Real photos (six small web photos) through `white`, `solid` and `set`.
- **Live plate generation** with `flux-3-image`: 13 plates (5 scene, 3 accessories, 5 display) for $0.312 total,
  composited with real photos into scene, accessories and display images. Details, prompts and costs:
  [plate-report.md](plate-report.md).
- Ring cushion and earring card were only checked with a **stand-in product** (a pendant), to verify slot,
  anchor and occlusion on the real plates. A real ring photo (front view, upright) and a real earring pair
  photo are still needed.

Not checked: high-resolution customer photos, other plate models, and how convincing the shadows look on
photos with very different lighting.

Observed: with the never-upscale rule, a small photo (225 px) looks tiny on a 1024 px plate. Photos of 1500 px or
more are shrunk to fit the slot instead, which is the intended case.
